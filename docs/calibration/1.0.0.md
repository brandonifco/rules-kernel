# Calibration for 1.0.0

Runs of `tools/calibration/run.sh` against the consumers pinned in
`tools/calibration/consumers.json` ([decision 0021](../decisions/0021-external-calibration-is-a-release-gate.md)).
If `src/RulesKernel/` or `src/RulesKernel.Analyzers/` changes again before the tag,
`tools/release-checks.py` rejects this record until it is rerun.

**What the consumers ran.** 1.0.0 renames `ReplayCompatibilityIdentity` to `EngineIdentity`
([decision 0022](../decisions/0022-the-pre-1.0-surface-review.md)), so no consumer at its
0.4.0 commit compiles against this tree. Each is pinned at a migration commit on its
`rules-kernel-1.0` branch: the pinned 0.4.0 commit plus the rename and nothing else, one to
three lines per engine. That is the entire migration a consumer makes to take 1.0.0.

**Fidelity.** The three factory-produced engines built with their own pinned SDK, 10.0.112.
SRD_Combat pins 8.0.129 with roll-forward disabled, and ran on 8.0.131 (`CALIBRATION_SDKS`).
`publish.yml` reruns the kernel mode at the tag.

## SRD_Combat against the kernel

Not a gate: SRD_Combat is an analyzer consumer in the manifest, because `publish.yml` cannot
install its pinned 8.0.129 SDK. It is recorded because it is the materially different consumer.
It is hand-written rather than factory-produced, targets net8.0 only, and draws through
`IRandomSource` in 91 places. Run at 4f6958f against kernel commit a2c11c7:

| consumer | commit | pins | tests | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SRD_Combat | 4f6958f | 1 | 5661 of 5662 | one environmental failure |

The failure is `SpriteGeometryTests.ShippedSheets_MatchTheCommittedGeometryManifest`, a viewer
test that shells out to `git ls-files`. `run.sh` copies a consumer's files without its `.git`,
so the test reports "not a git repository". It passes in SRD_Combat's own checkout at 847b310.
The build and every other test across its seven test projects pass: 5661 passed, and two
are skipped by design.

## What each consumer answered

Read from the four migrated commits above.

- **Identity.** Each constructs `EngineIdentity` with no change beyond its name, and each pins
  one corpus. hoyle-backgammon and srd-52-combat declare `Pcg32SetSeq64XshRr32`, faa-part-107
  declares no generator, and SRD_Combat declares its own `RandomAlgorithmId("system-random-seeded")`.
  So the identity names a generator the kernel does not provide as readily as one it does.
- **Unresolved results.** faa-part-107, hoyle-backgammon and srd-52-combat return
  `Resolution<T>` throughout (90, 153 and 147 uses) with the five reasons and no domain
  additions. None derives from `Resolution<T>`, so decision 0023 costs them nothing.
  SRD_Combat does not use it.
- **Provenance.** Each factory engine cites through `SourceLocator` in its corpus's own grammar,
  `§` designations or page-marked sections, and carries resolved citations in its own result
  types, as decision 0022 leaves them. None needed the kernel to parse a citation.
- **Randomness.** hoyle-backgammon and srd-52-combat draw through `IRandomSource`, `Pcg32` and
  `UniformInt`, and their draw-count tests pass unchanged. SRD_Combat implements
  `IRandomSource` itself (81 uses) over its own generator.
- **Analyzer.** Zero findings in the factory engines. SRD_Combat's 17 are the ones decision
  0015 read as true statements about its code, including RK0001 on the `System.Random` its
  identity already declares.
- **Did 1.0 move engine semantics into the kernel?** No. It added no type and no member. It
  renamed one type, closed one hole, and removed three unused types.
